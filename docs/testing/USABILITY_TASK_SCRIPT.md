# GadgetVault — Usability Session Script

Read the italic parts aloud. Everything else is for the facilitator.

---

## Before you start

- [ ] App running, seeded, fresh browser profile
- [ ] Consent form signed
- [ ] Observation sheet with today's date and the participant number on it
- [ ] Test cards visible to the participant (§ Test cards, below)

---

## Introduction (2 minutes)

> *Thanks for helping with this. This is a university project — an online shop
> called GadgetVault. I'm going to give you a few things to try to do on it.*
>
> *The most important thing: I'm not testing you. I'm testing the site. If
> something is confusing or you can't find something, that's exactly what I
> need to know — it means we built it wrong, not that you did anything wrong.*
>
> *Please say what you're thinking as you go. What you're looking for, what you
> expect a button to do, anything that surprises you. If you go quiet I'll just
> ask "what are you thinking?" — that's all that means.*
>
> *I can't help you while you're doing a task, even if you get stuck. That's not
> me being difficult; if I help, we lose the finding. You can stop any task or
> the whole session at any point, no reason needed.*
>
> *Nothing here is real. No real money moves, there's no real card involved,
> and I'll give you fake card numbers to type in. Any questions before we start?*

## Task 1 — Find and buy something (goal G1)

> *You want to buy a pair of headphones. Start from the home page and buy one —
> whatever you like the look of. You'll need to make an account along the way;
> use the details on this card rather than your own.*

Hand over a slip with a made-up name and an `@sen371.test` email address.

**Done when:** they reach the order confirmation page.

Watch for:
- Do they use the search box, the navigation, or the home page tiles?
- Do they find their way from a product to the cart?
- Do they hesitate at the sign-in wall, and do they understand why it appeared?
- Anything they say at the payment screen.

## Task 3 — A card that gets declined (goals G2, G3)

> *Buy something else, but this time use this card number.*

Give them **4000 0000 0000 9995** (declines, insufficient funds).

**Done when:** they have read the failure message and said what they would do.

Then ask:
> *In your own words — what just happened? What would you do next? Do you think
> you've been charged?*

This is the most valuable question in the session. Write down their answer word
for word, not a summary of it.

## Task 5 — Admin: add a product (goal G4)

Sign them out and sign in as an admin account first (details on the facilitator
sheet — the participant does not need them).

> *You now work at this shop. A new keyboard has arrived and it needs to go on
> the website. Put it on there. Call it whatever you like and price it at R899.*

**Done when:** the product is saved and visible in the catalogue.

Watch for:
- Do they find "Manage Catalogue" in the navigation?
- Do they know a category is needed first, if none fits?
- Do they type the price as 899 or as 89900? What does the field lead them to?
- The image field: do they understand what goes in it?

## Closing questions (3 minutes)

1. > *What was the most confusing part of that?*
2. > *Was there anything you expected to be there that wasn't?*
3. > *If this were a real shop, would you have finished that first purchase?*
   > *If not, where would you have given up?*
4. > *Anything else?*

> *That's everything — thank you. Everything you said gets written up without
> your name on it.*

## Facilitator reminders

- Do not answer "where do I click?" — answer with *"what would you try?"*
- Do not defend the design. Write down the complaint and move on.
- Silence is data. Let it run for a few seconds before prompting.
- Note the exact words. "I don't know if that worked" is a finding;
  "was unsure about confirmation" is a paraphrase that has lost the finding.
